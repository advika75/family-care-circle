# Final MTSamples Pending Policy Review Checklist

**For human review only. This document recommends no approval and does not authorize edits to existing gold.** The approved v1.0 policy leaves checklist items 3–5, 7, 9, and 11–17 pending. Use exactly the six labels `MEDICATION`, `DOSAGE`, `FREQUENCY`, `DURATION`, `CONDITION`, and `SYMPTOM`.

Examples labeled **verified gold** below were checked against the corresponding source file: the saved entity text equals `source[start:end]`. A source-only example is not claimed to be a gold annotation. Recommendations remain proposals until the project owner records a decision.

For each decision, select one: **[ ] Adopt recommendation  [ ] Reject; record replacement  [ ] Needs discussion**.

## 1. CONDITION shorthand: `diabetic` (checklist item 3)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §3 CONDITION, includes explicitly stated diagnoses but leaves diagnostic shorthand pending. The proposed draft says to label `diabetic` only when it clearly asserts a diagnosis, preserve the stated span, and not expand it to an unstated name. Shared guide `data/gold/README.md` says CONDITION covers diagnoses; it does not define adjectival shorthand.
- **Project example:** In source `data/public/mtsamples/notes/mtsamples_Acute_Inferior_Myocardial_Infarction.txt`, the sentence reads “The patient is diabetic and has a prior history of coronary artery disease.” **Verified gold:** note `mtsamples_Acute_Inferior_Myocardial_Infarction`, CONDITION `[169,177)` `diabetic`. A nearby contrast is **verified gold** CONDITION `[1062,1084)` `diabetic gastroparesis` in `mtsamples_Multiple_Medical_Problems_-_Discharge_Summary_064`, where the diagnosis name is explicit.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Keep the exact span `diabetic` as CONDITION when the text asserts the patient's diagnosed disease state; do not expand it. Send adjectival use that does not clearly assert a diagnosis to review.
- **Gold impact:** The `[169,177)` judgment could change if “is diabetic” is not accepted as an explicit diagnosis. The `diabetic gastroparesis` example is a distinct, explicit diagnosis and is not the same boundary question.
- **Choices:** Adopting the proposal makes clear diagnostic shorthand eligible without inferred expansion; rejecting it in favor of named diagnoses only may remove or relabel `[169,177)`; discussion leaves such shorthand unresolved.

## 2. Coordinated entities: split or retain (checklist item 4)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §2.2, approves shortest-complete spans, but expressly leaves coordinated segmentation pending. The proposed draft/checklist recommends separate spans for independent coordinated mentions. Shared guide requires every occurrence but does not specify coordinated segmentation.
- **Project examples:** The source sentence in `mtsamples_Acute_Inferior_Myocardial_Infarction.txt` says “He admits some shortness of breath & diaphoresis.” **Verified gold:** SYMPTOM `[550,583)` is the single span `shortness of breath & diaphoresis`. Another source says “The patient denies any fever or chills”; **verified gold** SYMPTOM `[669,683)` is `ever or chills` (the next item addresses its boundaries). For CONDITIONS, `mtsamples_Escharotomy` has **verified gold** CONDITION `[54,122)` `burn to the bilateral upper extremities and neck and anterior thorax`; the source describes one burn diagnosis across several locations, so it is not clearly a list of separate diagnoses.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Split coordinated terms only when each names an independent entity; keep a single diagnosis with coordinated locations as one entity when the locations modify one diagnosis. Exclude the conjunction from separate spans. Confirm whether this applies to all six labels.
- **Gold impact:** The combined SYMPTOM `[550,583)` would be replaced by two spans if the split rule is adopted. The Escharotomy span should not be split solely because it contains “and”; it is a boundary example for the independent-entity test. The documents do not identify a complete count of affected coordinated spans.
- **Choices:** Adopt to separate independent mentions and improve consistent counting; reject to retain a combined-list convention (which could preserve conjunctions in a span); discussion holds coordinated cases pending review.

## 3. Conjunctions, whitespace, and punctuation (checklist item 5)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §2.1, specifies exact source character offsets but leaves punctuation and conjunction boundaries pending. The proposed draft says to exclude conjunctions and outer punctuation/space while preserving punctuation that belongs to abbreviations. Shared guide documents exact source offsets but not these boundary details.
- **Project examples:** Source “fever or chills” appears in the AMI note as “denies any fever or chills.” **Verified gold:** SYMPTOM `[669,683)` `ever or chills`, which is a valid source slice but omits the initial `f` and includes `or`. **Verified gold:** SYMPTOM `[622,643)` is `' episodes of vomiting'`, including a leading space; source: “nausea & 3 episodes of vomiting tonight.” **Verified gold:** FREQUENCY `[5934,5940)` `b.i.d.` in `mtsamples_Bunionectomy_Osteotomy`; the period is part of the abbreviation token in the saved span.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Exclude conjunctions and external whitespace/punctuation; preserve abbreviation-internal punctuation. Where an abbreviation's period coincides with sentence-final punctuation, require an explicit consistent boundary decision.
- **Gold impact:** Both AMI SYMPTOM spans above are directly identified boundary candidates. The shared approved shortest-complete rule also supplies a specific example favoring `vomiting` over framing `episodes of vomiting`; any change must still be reviewed and recorded as a new gold version. The Bunionectomy abbreviation is a verified positive boundary example, not a demonstrated error.
- **Choices:** Adopt to regularize exact spans and avoid clipped/list-spanning labels; reject only with a replacement convention; discussion leaves edge punctuation and the identified gold spans unresolved.

## 4. Negated, historical, and watch-for SYMPTOM mentions (items 7 and 15)

- **Existing rule and source:** Shared guide `data/gold/README.md` says every occurrence, including negated mentions, is annotated and SYMPTOM includes reported or explicitly watched-for symptoms. Approved policy v1.0, §2.1, leaves the expanded negated/historical/watch-for and procedural-context details pending. The proposed draft includes negated and historical mentions, includes explicit named watch-for symptoms, excludes procedural events unless they independently describe a patient symptom, and forbids inferring symptoms from hypothetical complications.
- **Project examples:** The AMI source says “He denies any fever or chills.” Its **verified gold** SYMPTOM `[669,683)` `ever or chills` is negated in context, though its boundary is defective. In `mtsamples_Total_Thyroid_Lumpectomy.txt`, the source says “the small bleeding vessels were identified and bipolared”; no corresponding gold SYMPTOM span was found for that phrase. A source-only explicit instruction appears in `mtsamples_Well-Child_Check_-_5.txt`: “Call the office or on-call physician if the patient has fever, feeding problems or breathing problems.” No selected gold annotation file for that note was found, so this is not an existing gold case.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Treat mention status separately from assertion status: retain a stated symptom when negated or historical; include a named watch-for symptom; exclude objective/procedural wording unless it independently describes a patient symptom; do not infer a symptom from a possible complication that names no symptom.
- **Gold impact:** The AMI gold mention is a verified negated example and has a separate boundary issue. The thyroid procedural phrase is source-only, not a gold span. The watch-for example is not in the selected gold set. The reviewed documents do not identify a particular historical gold span whose inclusion would change.
- **Choices:** Adopt to keep explicit symptom mentions regardless of negation/history and to distinguish symptoms from operative findings; reject with a defined alternative for assertion status and watch-for text; discussion keeps ambiguous quotes, historical context, and prospective instructions unresolved.

## 5. DURATION boundaries and context-only intervals (checklist item 9)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §3 DURATION, already approves including treatment/care-instruction durations and excluding symptom timelines, follow-up intervals, and procedure/test elapsed time. Its exact boundary rules for number forms, modifiers, and context-only intervals remain pending. The shared guide gives `10 days` in “x 10 days” and `4 more days` as treatment/instruction duration; excludes `2 days of dysuria` and “follow up in 2 weeks.”
- **Project examples:** In `mtsamples_Multiple_Medical_Problems_-_Discharge_Summary_064`, source says the patient “was given three additional days of p.o. Levaquin” and “three additional days of Bactrim”; **verified gold** DURATION `[3145,3166)` and `[3202,3223)` each contain `three additional days`. In `mtsamples_Acute_Inferior_Myocardial_Infarction`, source says “The most recent episode of pain has lasted one-hour”; **verified gold** DURATION `[1123,1132)` is `one-hour.` In `mtsamples_Tilt_Table_Test_-_1`, **verified gold** DURATION `[297,307)` and `[405,415)` are `20 minutes` in descriptions of lying/standing on the tilt table.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** For boundary details, capture the interval and its meaningful number/modifier, excluding a separate marker such as `x` and a head noun such as `course`. Do not infer a treatment target from an unexplained interval; use context only when it identifies the treatment/care instruction unambiguously. Apply the already approved exclusions above.
- **Gold impact:** The two `three additional days` spans are treatment-duration examples consistent with item 8's approved scope. The `one-hour.` symptom timeline and both tilt-table `20 minutes` procedure/test spans conflict with the already approved DURATION exclusions and are verified review candidates for a separately documented gold adjudication; this checklist does not change them. The boundary rule could also affect intervals whose framing or modifiers are included.
- **Choices:** Adopt the proposed exact-interval boundary and unambiguous-target threshold; reject with a replacement rule for contextual intervals; discussion leaves ambiguous spans unresolved. Any existing gold change requires separate human adjudication and a new gold version.

## 6. DOSAGE medication concentrations (checklist item 11)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §3 DOSAGE, retains the shared-guide amount-plus-unit baseline but leaves medication concentrations pending. Shared guide excludes unrelated vitals/labs/percentages, e.g. `8.9%`; draft proposes including concentration only when it specifies an administered medication.
- **Project examples:** In `mtsamples_Bunionectomy_Osteotomy`, **verified gold** DOSAGE `[1554,1556)` `1%` appears in “1% Lidocaine plain was injected into the foot.” In `mtsamples_Carotid_Cerebral_Arteriograms`, **verified gold** DOSAGE `[644,646)` `1%` appears in “Lidocaine 1% ... was used as local anesthetic.” A contrasting source-only value in `mtsamples_2-D_Echocardiogram_-_1` is LVEF `51%`; it is not annotated as DOSAGE.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Include a percentage as DOSAGE only when source context explicitly identifies it as a medication concentration; exclude unrelated measurement percentages and do not infer an unclear relationship.
- **Gold impact:** The two verified gold concentration spans are clear medication contexts under the proposal and would remain. The diagnostic report's six `1%` DOSAGE FNs are detector/gold mismatches and do not show that the corresponding gold spans are incorrect; each source context would need review. The LVEF percentage is source-only, not gold DOSAGE.
- **Choices:** Adopt to distinguish concentrations from measurement percentages; reject by excluding concentrations or defining another rule (which may alter gold inclusion); discussion holds ambiguous percentages.

## 7. MEDICATION names, ingredients, and classes (checklist items 12–13)

- **Existing rule and source:** Shared guide allows specific drug names and name components, excludes broad classes such as `antibiotics`, `NSAIDs`, and `oral steroid`. Approved policy v1.0, §3 MEDICATION, labels name/class/ingredient guidance pending. Proposed draft includes products/generics/ingredients used as medication and leaves context-sensitive cases for review.
- **Project examples:** **Verified gold** MEDICATION in `mtsamples_Acute_Inferior_Myocardial_Infarction` `[1708,1726)` is `Humulin N. insulin`; source lists “Humulin N. insulin 50 units in a.m.” **Verified gold** in `mtsamples_Fat_Harvesting` `[786,801)` is `regular insulin`, in a preparation containing 100 units of regular insulin. **Verified gold** in `mtsamples_Multiple_Medical_Problems_-_Discharge_Summary_064` `[480,487)` is standalone `insulin`, in “diabetes mellitus requiring insulin for 26 years.” **Verified gold** in `mtsamples_Pectoralis_Tendon_Repair` `[2648,2672)` is `prophylactic antibiotics`, while the source describes receiving antibiotics. **Verified gold** `cortisone` `[1198,1207)` in `mtsamples_Total_Knee_Replacement` appears alongside anti-inflammatory medications and Synvisc. Specific named examples include verified gold `Marcaine`, `Lamictal`, `Levaquin`, and `Synthroid` in the listed note files and in the policy/checklist examples.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Include a specific product or generic medication/ingredient when context uses it as medication; exclude a broad therapeutic class. For a specific product followed by a generic descriptor, annotate the product alone when the descriptor is not part of its name. Decide contextually, not from a word list.
- **Gold impact:** The `Humulin N. insulin` span is a verified boundary candidate. `prophylactic antibiotics` is a verified gold span that appears to conflict with the shared guide's class exclusion; human review must determine whether the phrase denotes a class or a medication category before any change. The standalone/regular insulin and cortisone examples show why context matters but do not by themselves establish errors. The diagnostic report's `insulin` FPs and named-medication FNs likewise do not prove gold is wrong.
- **Choices:** Adopt the product/generic-versus-class distinction to preserve specific names while excluding classes; reject with a different class/ingredient rule (which may change the listed gold spans); discussion holds `insulin`, `cortisone`, `antibiotics`, and descriptor boundaries for source-level review.

## 8. FREQUENCY expressions and route exclusions (checklist item 14)

- **Existing rule and source:** Shared guide defines FREQUENCY as how often, gives `BID`, `q4-6h`, `once daily`, `every 4 hours`, `nightly`, `PRN`/`as needed`, and excludes routes such as `PO`. Approved policy v1.0, §3 FREQUENCY, retains abbreviation boundaries and non-medication scope as pending. The proposed draft includes cadence forms and says to preserve internal abbreviation punctuation but not a separate sentence-final period.
- **Project examples:** **Verified gold** FREQUENCY `b.i.d.` `[5934,5940)` in `mtsamples_Bunionectomy_Osteotomy` is in the medication instruction `Naprosyn one p.o. b.i.d.` **Verified gold** `QDay` `[1701,1705)` in the AMI note follows `Aspirin 81 milligrams`; **verified gold** `PRN` `[1796,1799)` occurs in `Nitroglycerin ... sublingually PRN chest pain`. `p.o.`/`PO` is a route in source instructions and is not labeled as FREQUENCY in these examples.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Keep cadence as FREQUENCY and route as excluded. Retain internal abbreviation punctuation; distinguish a separate sentence period. Apply FREQUENCY to non-medication instructions only when the phrase clearly expresses cadence; otherwise adjudicate.
- **Gold impact:** The cited verified frequency spans are examples of existing gold and align with the shared rule. The diagnostic report's `b.i.d.`, `q.d.`, and `QDay` FN forms and `daily` FP forms are evaluation mismatches, not grounds to alter gold. No verified gold FREQUENCY span was identified as a route; no gold change is established from these examples.
- **Choices:** Adopt to preserve cadence/route distinction and standardize abbreviation boundaries; reject with an alternative cadence inventory/scope; discussion leaves non-medication cadence and ambiguous final periods open.

## 9. Non-overlapping spans and tie-breaks (checklist item 16)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §2.3, explicitly leaves a flat/non-overlap policy pending. The shared guide does not specify overlapping labels; the MTSamples annotation tool rejects overlapping spans (as reported in `policy_consistency_review.md`).
- **Project examples:** The checklist cites competing examples `Chest pain`, `Humulin N insulin`, and `3 units per hour`. The first and the specified rate boundary have separate approved decisions (chest pain is SYMPTOM; the full rate is DOSAGE). A read-only check of current gold found **0 overlapping spans among 529 entities** and all entity text matched its source slice. The diagnostic report's 32 same-note FP/FN interval-overlap pairs compare predictions with gold; they are not overlapping gold annotations.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Keep gold spans flat and non-overlapping. Use approved semantic decisions first; for unresolved candidate overlaps, record candidates and defer to a human reviewer rather than choose automatically.
- **Gold impact:** No current gold overlap was found, so adopting the flat rule alone would not remove a verified overlapping gold pair. Boundary/label decisions can still change individual entities; the listed prediction/gold overlaps are evaluation disagreements, not proof of incorrect gold.
- **Choices:** Adopt to match the current tool and keep one label/span over each character; reject only if the project intends nested multi-label spans, which would require a separate schema/tool decision; discussion leaves conflict resolution blocked.

## 10. Unresolved cases, adjudication, and sign-off (checklist item 17)

- **Existing rule and source:** Approved policy v1.0, §§4–7, forbids automatic relabeling and says existing changes need individual review and a separately versioned gold set. It leaves the adjudication-log location and reviewer/sign-off practice to be established. The checklist proposes logging source span, candidate labels, rationale, and human approval.
- **Project examples:** Unresolved examples named in v1.0 include `diabetic`, `insulin`/`cortisone`/`antibiotics`, `Humulin N insulin`, `1%`, sentence-final abbreviation punctuation, quoted/procedural symptoms, ambiguous dosage rate/cadence, and time intervals without clear treatment targets. These are examples of review cases, not approvals.
- **Gold impact:** This decision governs any future change to the affected verified spans listed above; it does not itself classify any span. Policy v1.0 states existing gold has not thereby been reviewed under the policy.
- **Proposed resolution (not approved):** Before gold adjudication, choose one review log location and require note ID, exact source text/offsets, candidate labels from the six-label schema, rationale, reviewer, decision, date, and gold-version reference. Keep the existing 30-note gold snapshot intact; record any approved revisions in a new version and evaluate it separately.
- **Choices:** Adopt to make decisions reproducible and auditable; reject only with an equivalent documented process; discussion means no unresolved case should be changed yet.
