## What reading three outputs end to end showed (hand-written, one reader, not a clinical review)

* **demo.sqlite3#3 (synthetic NSTEMI note): wrong medicine details shown to the patient with no warning.** The summary
  says "the 2.5 mg dose of glyceryl trinitrate you take daily"; the note has glyceryl trinitrate 0.5 mg under the
  tongue only if chest pain occurs, and 2.5 mg belongs to ramipril. It also invents a four-day aspirin course ("once a
  day for the next 4 days. After that ... every day"); the note says aspirin 75 mg once daily, long term. No check
  fired: both "2.5 mg" and "4 days" occur somewhere in the note, so number matching cannot see that they are attached
  to the wrong drug. The caregiver fallback list also contains "aspirin, 4 days", which suggests the regimen grouping
  attached an unrelated duration to an aspirin mention and the prompt then asked the model to include it (likely, not
  traced).
* **note_04.txt (synthetic paediatric asthma note): mostly faithful draft, shown with a review warning, but with
  errors.** It reverses an instruction ("use it twice a day, after you rinse your mouth"; the note says rinse after use)
  and never says "2 puffs"; the pipeline then appended "2 puffs (dosage); as needed (frequency)" detached from the
  drug. The caregiver checklist is the research generic fallback plus "Confirm with the care team: 30 mg (dosage)",
  "BID (frequency)" and similar lines with no medicine name: the same mislabelled-appendix problem fixed for the
  patient view, still present in the caregiver view. The child text was in the third person ("The person ...") and
  passed the gate.
* **note_10.txt (synthetic migraine note): nothing generated reaches the patient or the child.** The patient summary
  was replaced by the claim gate (an unsupported recovery phrase) and the child text was withheld (recovery phrase and
  medication-change wording). Only the caregiver checklist, which is not gated this way, was shown.
